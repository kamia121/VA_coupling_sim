    Cases to work through

    Vasoplegia. Start norepinephrine at 0.1 µg/kg/min and watch MAP rise from 64 to 79 mmHg within ten minutes, while cardiac output barely changes and the LV Ea line steepens on the loop. Pmsf rises as well, from 8.2 to 9.2 mmHg, because norepinephrine constricts the capacitance veins and moves unstressed volume into the stressed pool . Two crystalloid boluses given together raise cardiac output from 9.4 to 10.3 L/min, and within 15 minutes of the end of the infusions it has fallen back to 9.7 L/min, as the capillary leak moves the fluid out of the vessels.

    Septic shock with depressed Ees. Norepinephrine alone restores MAP but lowers cardiac output and ScvO₂, because the weak ventricle ejects against the higher Ea. Adding dobutamine raises Ees, lowers Ea/Ees and narrows the PCO₂ gap. Epinephrine alone raises MAP and Ees together, and the lactate keeps rising for a reason unrelated to perfusion, the β₂-adrenergic stimulation of aerobic glycolysis. In the CATS trial, epinephrine alone and norepinephrine plus dobutamine gave the same 28-day mortality, 40% and 34% (P = 0.31) .

    Cardiogenic shock. The LV loop is wide and flat, LA pressure is high while CVP is normal, and the patient sits in the cold quadrant of the flow–congestion diagram. Dobutamine moves the loop to the left and raises the VTI within minutes. Milrinone takes hours, because its half-life is about 2 h . Norepinephrine raises MAP at the cost of stroke volume and a higher LA pressure, and nitroglycerin lowers LA pressure with little change in cardiac output.

    Hemorrhagic shock. Press Run and watch the first 45 minutes. Pmsf falls first and fastest: after 15 minutes and 450 mL, Pmsf is 23% lower, cardiac output 5% lower and MAP 4% lower. With 900 mL lost at 30 minutes, 18% of the blood volume, MAP is 86 mmHg; with 1,350 mL lost at 45 minutes, it is 75 mmHg. Hemoglobin hardly moves while the patient bleeds, because whole blood is lost; it falls later, as refill from the interstitium and crystalloid dilute the remaining red cells. Control the bleeding, then compare two units of red cells with a crystalloid bolus.

    Acute PE with RV ischemia. Fluid raises CVP and does little for cardiac output. Norepinephrine raises aortic pressure, restores RV coronary perfusion and lifts RV Ees, which reverses the ischemic spiral . Inhaled NO lowers the RV Ea directly.

    Tamponade. A crystalloid bolus raises every diastolic pressure and adds little flow. Draining 200 mL of pericardial fluid restores the loops within a tick.

    Dynamic LVOT obstruction. Raise dobutamine to 10 µg/kg/min and watch the LVOT gradient rise and MAP fall. Then stop it and start phenylephrine, or give esmolol with fluid. In a canine model, propranolol, phenylephrine and saline each reduced a dobutamine-induced gradient, from about 60 mmHg to 12 to 22 mmHg; phenylephrine raised aortic pressure most, and saline raised cardiac output most . Phenylephrine and β-blockers are used for the same obstruction in patients with shock .

    What the lab leaves out

    The circulation has no breathing, so there is no pulse pressure variation or passive leg raise. The baroreflex can raise heart rate by only 35% above the intrinsic rate, so the tachycardia of hemorrhage is smaller than in patients. There is no renin–angiotensin or vasopressin response, no kidney, and no coagulation. An intravascular volume change alters the stressed volume by 0.4 mL per mL, the remainder filling or emptying the unstressed veins. Lactate, capillary refill time and critical closing pressure are illustrative functions with the right direction of change, and their numbers are not calibrated to patients. Every drug acts through the same few targets (SVR, Ees, heart rate, venous tone, PVR, relaxation and critical closing pressure), with effect sizes chosen to give changes of the usual clinical order.
