About these values

Each tile gives a model value for the patient in the current state, and beneath it the change from the patient as found. A tile outlined in red is outside the usual range: mean arterial pressure below 65 mmHg, mean LA pressure above 18 mmHg, LVEDP above 16 mmHg, mean PA pressure above 20 mmHg or RA pressure above 12 mmHg. LA, LV, PA and RA pressures are transmural, measured against pleural pressure; the distinction matters only when the ventilator or the breathing trial is selected.

The chips beneath the tiles give the bedside consequences of those values. The Forrester subset combines cardiac index, low below 2.2 L/min/m² (cold), with PAWP, high above 18 mmHg (wet). The lungs are congested above a PAWP of 18 mmHg and in the range of alveolar edema above 25 mmHg. The systemic veins are congested with an RA pressure above 12 mmHg. Pulmonary hypertension is a mean PA pressure above 20 mmHg and is post-capillary when PAWP exceeds 15 mmHg.

Doppler echocardiogram

Mitral and pulmonary venous velocities are model flows divided by an orifice area. Tissue Doppler timing comes from the model, and the e′ amplitude is scaled from the fitted relaxation time constant (limitations).

Mitral inflow

Pulsed-wave Doppler at the tips of the mitral leaflets records the velocity of blood entering the LV, plotted above the baseline over successive beats, with the ECG beneath. The E wave is early filling. Its height follows the transmitral gradient at mitral valve opening, which rises with LA pressure and falls as LV relaxation slows. The A wave is filling by atrial contraction.

Slow relaxation lowers E and shifts filling to the atrial kick, so E/A falls below 0.8 (impaired relaxation). A raised LA pressure restores the early gradient and returns E/A to the normal range (pseudonormal). A stiff ventricle fills rapidly and briefly, so E is tall with a short deceleration time and A is small (restrictive). At fast heart rates the atrium contracts before early inflow has stopped; residual early flow then adds to A, and when inflow never decelerates between the two, a single wave is labeled E+A.

Tissue Doppler

Tissue Doppler at the lateral mitral annulus records the velocity of the myocardium rather than of blood. The annulus moves toward the apex in systole (s′, above the baseline) and back toward the atrium in early diastole (e′) and during atrial contraction (a′), both below the baseline.

e′ follows the rate of LV relaxation and changes relatively little with preload, so it stays low in every grade of dysfunction while mitral E rises and falls with LA pressure. The ratio E/e′ therefore rises with filling pressure. In the model, e′ is scaled from the fitted relaxation time constant τ and does not change with volume, which overstates its preload independence.

Pulmonary venous flow

Pulsed-wave Doppler in a pulmonary vein records flow into the LA. The systolic wave (S) occurs during ventricular systole, as the atrium relaxes and the AV plane descends. The diastolic wave (D) follows mitral valve opening and mirrors mitral E. Atrial reversal (Ar), below the baseline, is blood pushed back into the veins by atrial contraction.

As LA pressure rises and the atrium stiffens, systolic inflow falls relative to diastolic inflow, and S/D drops below 1. A large atrial reversal reflects atrial contraction into a full, stiff ventricle. The model's S wave is larger than in patients, so S/D is higher than clinical values in grades 0 to III.

Echo measurements

An asterisk marks a value beyond the ASE/EACVI 2016 cutoff , applied to lateral e′ and lateral E/e′ because the model has a single e′.

What this shows

These are the indices of the ASE/EACVI 2016 algorithm, measured from the model echocardiogram. E/A and deceleration time come from the mitral inflow, e′ and E/e′ from tissue Doppler, peak TR velocity from the RV–RA systolic gradient, and LA volume index from the largest LA volume of the beat.

An E/A of 0.8 or less with E of 50 cm/s or less indicates impaired relaxation with normal LA pressure, and an E/A of 2 or more indicates restrictive filling. Between those limits, two or more of E/e′ above 13, TR velocity above 2.8 m/s and LA volume index above 34 mL/m² indicate a raised LA pressure. An E velocity at A onset above 20 cm/s means that A includes residual early inflow. The PAWP estimate applies the Nagueh regression to E/e′, and the model's mean LA pressure beneath it is the quantity these indices estimate.

Echocardiographic classification of the virtual patients

Rows are the model grades and columns the grade assigned by the ASE/EACVI 2016 algorithm from each patient's model echocardiogram . The algorithm has no grade IV, the restrictive pattern that persists after preload reduction . A pattern is not graded when E and A form a single wave, because early inflow does not decelerate before atrial contraction, or when there is no measurable A wave.

What this shows

Each row counts the patients of one model grade by the grade that the echocardiographic algorithm assigns them. Counts on the diagonal agree with the underlying stage; counts off it are patients near a grade boundary whose pattern resembles the neighboring grade. The second table repeats the classification after 1.5 L removed: grade II patients who move to grade I show the impaired relaxation beneath the pseudonormal pattern, and grade III and IV patients who leave grade III have reversible restrictive filling.

Fluid followed by diuresis

Reference patient of each grade: 1 L of crystalloid over 30 minutes, then diuresis of 500 mL/h from 60 to 240 minutes. The normal and grade I curves end when stressed volume reaches the lower limit of the model.

What this shows

The reference patient of each grade over five hours. Two 500 mL boluses of crystalloid are infused over the first 30 minutes, and diuresis of 500 mL/h runs from 60 to 240 minutes. Only 18% of crystalloid remains intravascular, and the rest leaves the circulation with a time constant of 10 minutes, so LA pressure rises during the infusion and drifts back before diuresis begins. In grades III and IV, the fluid raises LA pressure without changing cardiac output, and diuresis lowers LA pressure at a small cost in output. The E/A chart follows the mitral pattern over the same period: E/A falls from 1.37 to 0.84 in grade II and below the restrictive cutoff in grade III, while grade IV stays above it.
