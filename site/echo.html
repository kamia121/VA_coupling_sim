Coupling from echo

Coupling compares the contractility of the ventricle (Ees) with the load it ejects into (Ea). Echo cannot measure either one directly, so each index below is built from a few measurements, and the stations further down show how to take each of them. The This patient tables use the model's values until you make your own measurements, and then they update. The catheter values appear underneath each table.

LV: Ea/Ees

How to obtain it

  1. Measure the stroke volume as the LVOT area multiplied by the LVOT VTI (station 1) .
  2. Estimate Ea as 0.9 × cuff SBP / SV, because end-systolic pressure is about 90% of systolic pressure .
  3. Estimate Ees with the published single-beat method, which uses the cuff SBP and DBP, the stroke volume, the EF, and the ratio of the pre-ejection time (R wave to onset of flow) to the total systolic time (R wave to end of flow) on the LVOT Doppler .
  4. Alternatively, skip Ees and use the shortcut Ea/Ees ≈ ESV/SV = (1 − EF)/EF, which holds when V₀ is close to zero. The shortcut follows from the geometry of the loop, and it overstates the ratio when V₀ is large.

This patient

How to read it

Normal human hearts have an Ees/Ea of 1.62, which corresponds to an Ea/Ees of about 0.62 . In canine hearts, stroke work and efficiency stayed at 90% or more of their optimum for ratios between 0.3 and 1.3 . In a study of septic shock, uncoupling was defined as a ratio above 1.36 . After reading the ratio, compare Ea and Ees separately with normal to see which one moved. A normal ratio with both values raised is the pattern seen in HFpEF .

What it can change at the bedside

When Ea is high, as with vasoconstriction or hypertensive heart failure, vasodilators lower it. Rapid reduction of high blood pressure restores Ea and reverses the myocardial dysfunction . In HFpEF, however, the same vasodilation lowered blood pressure 2.6-fold more than in HFrEF and could reduce stroke volume .

When Ees is low, as in cardiomyopathy or septic myocardial depression, inotropes raise it . For septic shock with cardiac dysfunction and persistent hypoperfusion despite adequate volume and blood pressure, the Surviving Sepsis Campaign suggests adding dobutamine to norepinephrine or using epinephrine alone, as a weak recommendation .

When Ea is low and the patient is hypotensive, as in vasoplegia, norepinephrine is the first-line vasopressor, with an initial MAP target of 65 mmHg . Norepinephrine raises Ea , and in hypotensive postoperative patients it increased stroke volume only when coupling was altered .

Coupling explains the hemodynamics, but it has not been validated as a treatment target, and the concept "still needs to be tested in large trials" . The estimate also has several sources of error. An error in the LVOT diameter is squared in the stroke volume, 0.9 × SBP only approximates the end-systolic pressure, and the EF shortcut fails when the ESPVR is shifted to the right, as in HFrEF.

RV: TAPSE/PASP and SV/ESV

How to obtain it

  1. Measure TAPSE by M-mode through the lateral tricuspid annulus (station 3). TAPSE is a length-based index of RV contraction.
  2. Estimate the load as PASP = 4 × (peak TR velocity)² + RAP (station 2) .
  3. Divide TAPSE by PASP to obtain TAPSE/PASP in mm/mmHg, an index of function relative to load .
  4. If 3D echo or MRI is available, calculate SV/ESV from the RV volumes (station 4). SV/ESV approximates Ees/Ea when V₀ is close to zero .

This patient

How to read it

In PAH and CTEPH, a TAPSE/PASP below 0.31 predicted an Ees/Ea below 0.805 , the level at which the RV dilates and fails . In heart failure, a value below 0.36 carried a worse prognosis . For risk in PAH, a value above 0.32 indicates low risk and a value below 0.19 indicates high risk . An SV/ESV of 0.515 or less predicted a worse outcome . A low TAPSE/PASP can come from a low TAPSE or from a high PASP, so both parts of the ratio should be read.

What it can change at the bedside

In chronic PAH, TAPSE/PASP is one of the non-invasive variables in the risk assessment that the ESC/ERS 2022 guidelines use at follow-up .

In acute RV failure, a low ratio usually comes with a rising RAP and a falling MAP. Treating the cause comes first, together with judicious fluid management, inotropes and vasopressors, assist devices, and RV-protective ventilation . Inhaled pulmonary vasodilators can also be considered . These interventions have not been well studied .

In pulmonary hypertension due to left heart disease, a low ratio carries risk in heart failure . However, PAH drugs are not recommended in group 2 PH, and treatment is directed at the left heart disease .

Doppler and catheter measurements of PASP differed by more than 10 mmHg in 48% of 65 patients . TAPSE measures only longitudinal motion and depends on the angle of the cursor. SV/ESV reads low when V₀ is large, as in decompensated PAH.

Station 1 · Stroke volume from the LVOT VTI

  1. View. Use the apical five-chamber view.
  2. Cursor. Place the pulsed-wave sample volume in the LVOT just below the aortic valve, parallel to flow.
  3. Measure. Trace the modal velocity envelope of one beat to obtain the VTI, and measure the LVOT diameter in the parasternal long-axis view in mid-systole. Stroke volume is SV = π(D/2)² × VTI . and Ea is approximately 0.9 × SBP / SV .

Station 2 · PASP from the TR jet

  1. View. Use the RV-focused apical four-chamber view or the parasternal RV inflow view.
  2. Cursor. Align the continuous-wave Doppler cursor with the TR jet.
  3. Measure. Read the peak velocity at the edge of the dense envelope. PASP is 4v² + RAP, with RAP estimated from the size and collapse of the IVC .

Station 3 · TAPSE and TAPSE/PASP

  1. View. Use the RV-focused apical four-chamber view.
  2. Cursor. Place the M-mode cursor through the lateral tricuspid annulus, parallel to the RV free wall.
  3. Measure. Measure the annular excursion from end-diastole to peak systole, and divide it by PASP.

Click the annulus at end-diastole, where it is lowest, and then at peak systole, where it is highest. Drag either mark to adjust it.

The values 0.19 and 0.32 are the prognostic tertiles in PAH that the ESC/ERS 2022 risk table uses . A value of 0.31 predicted an Ees/Ea below 0.805 in PAH , and 0.36 is the risk threshold in heart failure . A TAPSE below 18 mm was associated with lower survival in pulmonary hypertension .

In the model, TAPSE scales linearly with RV stroke volume and is 22 mm in the normal heart. A real TAPSE measures only longitudinal motion and depends on the angle of the cursor.

Station 4 · RV volumes and SV/ESV

  1. Modality. Use 3D echo or cardiac MRI.
  2. Frames. Mark end-diastole, where the RV is largest, and end-systole, where it is smallest.
  3. Compute. Calculate SV/ESV, which approximates Ees/Ea when V₀ is close to zero . A value of 0.515 or less predicted a worse outcome .

Other RV findings

Other findings include the size of the RV relative to the LV, septal flattening in systole (pressure overload) or diastole (volume overload), fractional area change, tissue Doppler S′, RA size, and the IVC . Mid-systolic notching of the RVOT Doppler envelope was 96% specific for a PVR above 5 WU . The Chen single-beat method for Ees uses cuff pressures, Doppler stroke volume, EF, and an estimated normalized elastance .
