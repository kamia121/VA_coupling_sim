  1. View Apical five-chamber.
  2. Cursor Pulsed-wave sample volume in the LVOT, just below the aortic valve, parallel to flow.
  3. Measure Trace the modal velocity envelope of one beat (VTI). LVOT diameter from the parasternal long axis in mid-systole. SV = π(D/2)² × VTI . Ea ≈ 0.9 × SBP / SV .

3 · TAPSE and TAPSE/PASP

  1. View RV-focused apical four-chamber.
  2. Cursor M-mode through the lateral tricuspid annulus, parallel to the RV free wall.
  3. Measure Annular excursion from end-diastole to peak systole. Divide by PASP.

Click the annulus at end-diastole (lowest), then at peak systole (highest). Drag to adjust.

0.19 and 0.32: prognostic tertiles in PAH , used in the ESC/ERS 2022 risk table . 0.31: predicted Ees/Ea < 0.805 in PAH . 0.36: risk threshold in heart failure . TAPSE < 18 mm: lower survival in PH .

Model TAPSE scales linearly with RV stroke volume (normal 22 mm). Real TAPSE is longitudinal motion only and angle-dependent.

4 · RV volumes → SV/ESV

  1. Modality 3D echo or cardiac MRI.
  2. Frames End-diastole (largest) and end-systole (smallest).
  3. Compute SV/ESV ≈ Ees/Ea, assuming V₀ ≈ 0 . ≤ 0.515 predicted worse outcome .

With a right-shifted ESPVR (large V₀), SV/ESV reads below the true Ees/Ea. Compare the two in decompensated PAH.

EF as a coupling index

With V₀ ≈ 0, Ea/Ees ≈ ESV/SV = (1 − EF)/EF. EF 60% → 0.67; EF 40% → 1.5; EF 25% → 3. EF falls when Ea rises at constant Ees. The approximation fails with large V₀ and cannot separate high Ea from low Ees.

Other RV findings

RV size relative to LV, systolic (pressure) or diastolic (volume) septal flattening, fractional area change, tissue Doppler S′, RA size and IVC . Mid-systolic notching of the RVOT Doppler envelope: 96% specific for PVR > 5 WU . Chen single-beat Ees uses cuff pressures, Doppler SV, EF and an estimated normalized elastance .
