Coupling from echo

Neither Ees nor Ea can be measured directly by echo; each index below is derived from two or three measurements obtained at the stations further down the page. The This patient tables show the model values until a station is measured and then switch to the measured values, with the catheter values beneath each table.

Left ventricle (Ea/Ees)

Measurement

  1. Stroke volume is the LVOT area multiplied by the LVOT VTI (station 1) .
  2. Ea is estimated as 0.9 × cuff SBP / SV, since end-systolic pressure is approximately 90% of systolic pressure .
  3. Ees is estimated with the single-beat method, which uses cuff SBP and DBP, stroke volume, EF, and the ratio of the pre-ejection time (R wave to onset of flow) to the total systolic time (R wave to end of flow) on the LVOT Doppler .
  4. Without an estimate of Ees, Ea/Ees can be approximated as ESV/SV = (1 − EF)/EF, which assumes that V₀ is close to zero and overestimates the ratio when V₀ is large.

This patient

Interpretation

In one study of normal human hearts, mean Ees/Ea was 1.62, corresponding to Ea/Ees of about 0.62 . In isolated canine hearts, stroke work and oxygen-use efficiency stayed within 90% of their maxima across Ea/Ees values from 0.3 to 1.3; that is an experimental near-optimal range, not a clinical normal range . One septic-shock study defined uncoupling as Ea/Ees above 1.36; this cohort threshold is not a universal treatment target . Interpret the ratio alongside the absolute elastances, filling pressure, and cardiac output: HFpEF can have a similar ratio while both elastances are high .

Implications for management

A high Ea, as in vasoconstriction or hypertensive heart failure, is reduced by vasodilators, and rapid reduction of an elevated blood pressure restores Ea and reverses the myocardial dysfunction . In HFpEF, the same vasodilation lowered blood pressure 2.6-fold more than in HFrEF and could reduce stroke volume .

A low Ees, as in cardiomyopathy or septic myocardial depression, is increased by inotropes . For septic shock with cardiac dysfunction and persistent hypoperfusion despite adequate fluid resuscitation and arterial pressure, the 2026 Surviving Sepsis Campaign conditionally suggests adding dobutamine to norepinephrine or using epinephrine alone; the evidence is of very low certainty .

For septic shock with vasoplegia, the 2026 Surviving Sepsis Campaign guidelines recommend norepinephrine as the first-line vasopressor and an initial MAP target of 65 mmHg . Norepinephrine increases Ea , and in hypotensive postoperative patients it increased stroke volume only when coupling was altered .

Ventricular–arterial coupling has not been validated as a treatment target, and the concept "still needs to be tested in large trials" . An error in the LVOT diameter is squared in the stroke volume, 0.9 × SBP only approximates end-systolic pressure, and the EF-based estimate is inaccurate when the ESPVR is shifted to the right, as in HFrEF.

Right ventricle (TAPSE/PASP and SV/ESV)

Measurement

  1. TAPSE is measured by M-mode through the lateral tricuspid annulus (station 3) and is a length-based index of RV contraction.
  2. PASP, a pressure-based surrogate for RV load, is estimated as 4 × (peak TR velocity)² + RAP (station 2) .
  3. TAPSE/PASP, in mm/mmHg, relates RV function to its load .
  4. When 3D echo or MRI is available, SV/ESV is calculated from the RV volumes (station 4) and approximates Ees/Ea if V₀ is close to zero .

This patient

Interpretation

In PAH and CTEPH, a TAPSE/PASP below 0.31 predicted an Ees/Ea below 0.805 , the level associated with RV dilation and failure . In heart failure, a value below 0.36 was associated with a worse prognosis . In PAH, a value above 0.32 indicates low risk and a value below 0.19 indicates high risk . An SV/ESV of 0.515 or less predicted a worse outcome . A low TAPSE/PASP may result from a low TAPSE, a high PASP, or both.

Implications for management

In chronic PAH, TAPSE/PASP is one of the non-invasive variables in the risk assessment used at follow-up in the ESC/ERS 2022 guidelines .

In acute RV failure, a low ratio is usually accompanied by a rising RAP and a falling MAP. Identification and treatment of the underlying cause comes first, together with judicious fluid management, inotropes and vasopressors, assist devices, and RV-protective ventilation , and inhaled pulmonary vasodilators may be considered . These interventions have not been well studied .

In pulmonary hypertension due to left heart disease, a low ratio is associated with risk in heart failure . Treatment focuses on the left heart disease. PAH drugs are not established therapy for group 2 PH; the 2022 ESC/ERS guideline makes no general recommendation for or against PDE5 inhibitors in HFpEF with combined post- and pre-capillary PH .

Doppler and catheter measurements of PASP differed by more than 10 mmHg in 48% of 65 patients . TAPSE measures only longitudinal motion and depends on the cursor angle. SV/ESV underestimates Ees/Ea when V₀ is large, as in decompensated PAH.

Station 1 · Stroke volume from the LVOT VTI

  1. View. Apical five-chamber view.
  2. Cursor. Pulsed-wave sample volume in the LVOT, just below the aortic valve and parallel to flow.

Station 3 · TAPSE and TAPSE/PASP

  1. View. RV-focused apical four-chamber view.
  2. Cursor. M-mode through the lateral tricuspid annulus, parallel to the RV free wall.
  3. Measurement. Annular excursion from end-diastole to peak systole, divided by PASP.

Click the annulus at end-diastole (lowest point) and at peak systole (highest point), and drag either mark to adjust it.

The cutoffs of 0.19 and 0.32 are the prognostic tertiles in PAH adopted in the ESC/ERS 2022 risk table . A value of 0.31 predicted an Ees/Ea below 0.805 in PAH , and 0.36 is the risk threshold in heart failure . A TAPSE below 18 mm was associated with lower survival in pulmonary hypertension .

In the model, TAPSE is proportional to RV stroke volume and is 22 mm in the normal heart. Measured TAPSE reflects only longitudinal motion and depends on the cursor angle.

Station 4 · RV volumes and SV/ESV

  1. Modality. 3D echo or cardiac MRI.
  2. Frames. End-diastole, when the RV is largest, and end-systole, when it is smallest.
  3. Calculation. SV/ESV approximates Ees/Ea when V₀ is close to zero . A value of 0.515 or less predicted a worse outcome .

Other RV findings

Other findings include RV size relative to the LV, septal flattening in systole (pressure overload) or diastole (volume overload), fractional area change, tissue Doppler S′, RA size, and IVC size and collapse . Mid-systolic notching of the RVOT Doppler envelope was 96% specific for a PVR above 5 WU . The Chen single-beat method for Ees uses cuff pressures, Doppler stroke volume, EF, and an estimated normalized elastance .
