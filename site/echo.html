Coupling from echo

Coupling compares contractility (Ees) with the load it ejects into (Ea). Echo does not measure either directly, so each index below is built from a few measurements. The stations further down show how to take each one. The This patient tables update as you measure and use the model's values until you do. The catheter truth sits underneath.

LV: Ea/Ees

How to get it

  1. Stroke volume = LVOT area × LVOT VTI (station 1) .
  2. Ea = 0.9 × cuff SBP / SV, because end-systolic pressure is about 90% of systolic .
  3. Ees by the published single-beat method uses cuff SBP and DBP, SV, EF, and the ratio of pre-ejection time (R wave to flow onset) to total systolic time (R wave to end of flow) on the LVOT Doppler .
  4. Shortcut without Ees: if V₀ ≈ 0, Ea/Ees ≈ ESV/SV = (1 − EF)/EF. This is algebra on the PV loop, and it overstates the ratio when V₀ is large.

This patient

How to read it

Normal human hearts: Ees/Ea 1.62, so Ea/Ees ≈ 0.62 . Stroke work and efficiency stay ≥ 90% of optimum from 0.3 to 1.3 (canine hearts) . In a septic shock study, uncoupling was defined as above 1.36 . Then ask which side moved by comparing Ea and Ees with normal. A normal ratio with both raised is the HFpEF pattern .

What it can change at the bedside

  • Ea high (vasoconstriction, hypertensive heart failure): vasodilators lower Ea. Rapidly reducing high blood pressure restores Ea and reverses the myocardial dysfunction . In HFpEF, the same vasodilation drops blood pressure 2.6-fold more than in HFrEF and can lower stroke volume .
  • Ees low (cardiomyopathy, septic depression): inotropes raise Ees . For septic shock with cardiac dysfunction and persistent hypoperfusion despite adequate volume and blood pressure, the guideline suggests adding dobutamine to norepinephrine or using epinephrine alone (weak recommendation) .
  • Ea low with hypotension (vasoplegia): norepinephrine is first-line, with an initial MAP target of 65 mmHg . Norepinephrine raises Ea . In hypotensive postoperative patients it raised stroke volume only when coupling was altered .

Evidence: coupling explains the haemodynamics but has not been validated as a treatment target; it "still needs to be tested in large trials" . Error sources: an error in LVOT diameter is squared in SV; 0.9 × SBP only approximates end-systolic pressure; the EF shortcut fails when the ESPVR is shifted right (try HFrEF).

RV: TAPSE/PASP and SV/ESV

How to get it

  1. TAPSE by M-mode through the lateral tricuspid annulus (station 3): a length-based index of RV contraction.
  2. PASP = 4 × (peak TR velocity)² + RAP (station 2) : the load.
  3. TAPSE/PASP in mm/mmHg: function divided by load .
  4. Volume method: SV/ESV from 3D echo or MRI (station 4) approximates Ees/Ea if V₀ ≈ 0 .

This patient

How to read it

In PAH and CTEPH, TAPSE/PASP below 0.31 predicted Ees/Ea below 0.805 , the level at which the RV dilates and fails . In heart failure, below 0.36 carried a worse prognosis . For PAH risk, above 0.32 is low and below 0.19 is high . SV/ESV ≤ 0.515 predicted a worse outcome . A low ratio can come from a low TAPSE or a high PASP, so read both parts.

What it can change at the bedside

  • Chronic PAH: TAPSE/PASP is one of the non-invasive variables in the ESC/ERS 2022 risk assessment at follow-up .
  • Acute RV failure (a low ratio with rising RAP and falling MAP): treating the cause comes first, with judicious fluid management, inotropes and vasopressors, assist devices and RV-protective ventilation . Inhaled pulmonary vasodilators can also be considered . These interventions have not been well investigated .
  • PH from left heart disease: a low ratio carries risk in heart failure , but PAH drugs are not recommended in group 2 PH; treat the left heart disease .

Error sources: Doppler and catheter PASP were more than 10 mmHg apart in 48% of 65 patients . TAPSE measures longitudinal motion only and depends on the cursor angle. SV/ESV reads low when V₀ is large (try decompensated PAH).

Station 3 · TAPSE and TAPSE/PASP

  1. View RV-focused apical four-chamber.
  2. Cursor M-mode through the lateral tricuspid annulus, parallel to the RV free wall.
  3. Measure Annular excursion from end-diastole to peak systole. Divide by PASP.
