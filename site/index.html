Ventricular–arterial coupling

Pressure–volume loops of the left and right ventricles generated by a physiological model. Contractility, load, and volume can be changed while the loop is compared with the normal ventricle.

Open the simulatorEcho lab

Concepts

What Ees and Ea mean, how their ratio sets stroke volume and efficiency, how the RV differs, and why a normal ratio can hide disease.

Simulator

Drag the loop, give fluid, vasopressors, inotropes, or pulmonary vasodilators, and replay each change step by step.

Scenarios

HFpEF, HFrEF, septic shock, compensated and decompensated PAH, acute PE, and combined pre- and post-capillary PH, each with its mechanism and the management evidence.

Echo lab

Learn how coupling is estimated by echo, then trace an LVOT VTI, measure the TR velocity and TAPSE, and mark RV volumes, and compare each result with the catheter values.

PA catheter

Float a catheter through each chamber, read the atrial waves and the wedge, recognize common artifacts, and calculate PVR, compliance, PAPi, and single-beat Ees/Ea.

Five-minute tour

  1. In the simulator, on the LV tab, drag the ESPVR handle down until Ees is about 1, then press Replay.
  2. Press Norepinephrine twice and compare the change in stroke volume with that in the normal heart.
  3. Press Show disease with an in-range Ea/Ees to see diseased ventricles whose ratio is still normal.
  4. On the RV tab, load Acute massive PE and then PAH, compensated. At a similar PVR, the unadapted RV in acute PE delivers a cardiac output of only 3.9 L/min, whereas the hypertrophied RV in compensated PAH maintains 5.2 L/min.
  5. In the echo lab, choose decompensated PAH, measure the TR velocity and TAPSE, and compare TAPSE/PASP with the model's Ees/Ea.

This is a teaching model. Its parameters are synthetic, it has no reflexes, and it does not make treatment recommendations.
