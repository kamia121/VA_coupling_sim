ICU diastology

Mean left atrial (LA) pressure in a critically ill patient is estimated at the bedside from mitral inflow indexed to a surrogate of myocardial relaxation. Mitral annular e′, the early diastolic velocity of the annulus, is the surrogate used in the ASE/EACVI algorithm . Color M-mode flow propagation velocity (Vp) is an alternative recorded within the LV cavity, and in the ICU it depends on systolic function and catecholamines as well as on relaxation.

Flow propagation velocity

Early diastolic inflow enters the LV as a jet whose leading edge travels from the mitral tips toward the apex. In an axisymmetric Navier–Stokes model of LV filling, the vortex formed during the acceleration of early filling determined the propagation of the maximal velocity on the simulated color M-mode . The front advances faster than the blood within it when relaxation and elastic recoil lower apical pressure below basal pressure. In dogs, propagation from the mitral tips to the apex far exceeded the velocity of the individual blood cells before ischemia and approximated it after coronary microembolization, which lengthened τ from 31 to 49 ms and lowered the mitral-to-apical pressure gradient from 1.9 to 0.7 mmHg .

Vp is the slope of the leading edge of the first aliasing contour, measured from the mitral valve plane to 4 cm into the cavity with the Nyquist limit lowered until the central jet aliases. It falls as relaxation slows: across inotropic states in dogs and in patients on partial cardiopulmonary bypass, Vp correlated with τ (r = 0.78 and 0.86) . In a heterogeneous population, systolic performance carries more weight. Among 150 patients undergoing coronary angiography, LV end-systolic volume index accounted for 46% of the variance of Vp and τ for 3% , so a small ventricle that empties well can sustain Vp while relaxation is impaired.

Vp is less reproducible than tissue Doppler. Interobserver variability was 14% for Vp and 7% for e′ , and in 40 critically ill patients E/Vp could be obtained in 72% against 92% for E/e′ . Applied to the same images, the three published definitions of Vp gave values that differed by as much as 250% .

E/Vp and mean LA pressure

E rises with mean LA pressure and falls as relaxation slows, so dividing E by a surrogate of relaxation leaves a ratio that varies with LA pressure. In 45 ICU patients with invasive monitoring, E/Vp estimated pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) as 5.27 × E/Vp + 4.6, with r = 0.80 and a standard error of the estimate of 3.1 mmHg. E alone correlated with PCWP at r = 0.62 and Vp at r = −0.34 .

Reduced ejection fraction

The dilated ventricle with an EF of 22% has a low Vp that changes little with volume, falling from 40 to 35 cm/s between 1 L removed and 1 L added, while E rises with LA pressure. E/Vp rises from 1.36 to 2.37 as mean LA pressure rises from 7 to 21 mmHg. Patients showed the same insensitivity of Vp to preload: during partial cardiopulmonary bypass, E fell from 69 to 53 cm/s while Vp went from 37 to 34 cm/s, a change that was not significant . With an EF below 50%, E/Vp correlated with LV end-diastolic pressure at r = 0.77 . Septic myocardial depression with an EF of 41% follows the same course. A liter of fluid raises mean LA pressure from 7 to 14 mmHg and E/Vp from 1.48 to 2.04, while Vp changes by 3 cm/s.

Lateral e′ falls with systolic dysfunction as well as with slow relaxation. After a first myocardial infarction, annular early diastolic velocity correlated with EF at r = 0.66 . In patients hospitalized with heart failure and a mean EF of 24.7%, e′ averaged over four annular sites was 6.2 ± 1.9 cm/s . The dilated ventricle has a lateral e′ of 7.3 cm/s. Across the volume steps, its estimate from E/e′ rises from 11 to 17 mmHg while mean LA pressure rises from 7 to 21 mmHg. The estimate from E/Vp covers nearly the same span, from 12 to 17 mmHg. In 106 patients with advanced decompensated heart failure and an EF of 30% or less, E/e′ did not correlate with PCWP, particularly in those with larger LV volumes, a lower cardiac index or cardiac resynchronization therapy . The model's e′ depends on τ and EF only and carries none of these sources of scatter, so its E/e′ rises with every rise in LA pressure.

Restrictive filling with a preserved ejection fraction

Both ratios underestimate a high filling pressure. In the restrictive ventricle with an EF of 55%, E/Vp gives 16.7 mmHg and E/e′ 18.0 mmHg for a mean LA pressure of 20.8 mmHg, in part because the restrictive E velocity of the model is lower than the velocities commonly recorded at the bedside. In patients, the underestimation arose from Vp. Six patients in a cardiac ICU with restrictive filling and preserved systolic function had a Vp of 71 ± 15 cm/s, against 37 ± 10 cm/s in the remainder, and their wedge pressure was largely underestimated .

Confounding of Vp in the ICU

A small, hyperdynamic ventricle maintains Vp while relaxation slows. With relaxation slowed to a fitted τ of 61 ms in septic vasoplegia, Vp remains at 50 cm/s and lateral e′ falls to 7.1 cm/s, below the ASE/EACVI cutoff of 10 cm/s.

Inotropic stimulation raises Vp independently of filling pressure. In patients without regional wall motion abnormalities, low-dose dobutamine raised Vp from 45 to 59 cm/s while E and E/Em did not change . With the standard inotrope dose, Vp in septic cardiomyopathy rises from 52 to 62 cm/s and mean LA pressure falls by less than 1 mmHg, so E/Vp falls for an almost unchanged LA pressure.

When EF is preserved, Vp changes with preload. In patients on hemodialysis, Vp fell after dialysis when EF was above 50% and did not change consistently when EF was lower , and ultrafiltration lowered Vp from 45 to 41 cm/s . Neither ratio follows change reliably within one patient. When PCWP was varied from 0.8 to 28.8 mmHg by lower-body negative pressure and saline in healthy subjects and patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, the individual slopes of PCWP on E/Vp ranged from −16.4 to 25.4 .

In these studies, E/Vp did not estimate filling pressure better than lateral E/e′ and was obtained in fewer patients. Its errors were largest where preserved systolic function or catecholamines raised Vp independently of LA pressure, a combination common in septic shock. Where Vp is least dependent on load, in the dilated, poorly emptying ventricle, E/Vp rose with filling pressure.

When mitral annular velocities cannot be recorded

Annular calcification, an annuloplasty ring or prosthesis, regional dysfunction at the annulus, and abnormal septal motion after cardiac surgery or with left bundle branch block uncouple annular motion from LV relaxation. Vp is recorded within the cavity and does not depend on annular motion, which makes it a candidate substitute in these patients. The evidence for that use is indirect. A PubMed search found no study of Vp in annular calcification or after annuloplasty. In 50 ICU patients, abnormal septal motion lowered the correlation of lateral E/Ea with PCWP from 0.86 to 0.36, and E/Vp correlated poorly with PCWP whether septal motion was normal or abnormal .

Limitations of the model

Vp is not simulated. The model has one LV compartment and no long axis, so the speed of the filling front cannot emerge from it. Vp is scaled from the fitted relaxation time constant and the ejection fraction, Vp = (36/τ) × [45 + 0.93 × (EF − 30)] cm/s, with τ in milliseconds, EF in percent and the term EF − 30 set to zero below an EF of 30%. These constants give the dilated ventricle a Vp of 38 cm/s and the normal ventricle 69 cm/s. In patients, the propagation rate measured by a related method was 34 ± 14 cm/s with an EF below 60% and an E/A below 1, and 74 ± 17 cm/s in controls .

At a fixed τ and EF, the scaled Vp does not change with volume, which overstates its preload independence when EF is preserved . The ejection fraction stands in for restoring forces as a whole and does not reproduce the high Vp of restrictive filling with preserved systolic function . The inotrope raises Ees and leaves τ unchanged, so the lusitropic effect of dobutamine is absent. In the dilated ventricle it raises E from 74 to 89 cm/s while mean LA pressure falls from 14 to 12 mmHg, and E/Vp rises.

Lateral e′ is scaled from τ, as in the Diastolic lab, and is reduced by 1% for each percentage point of EF below 50%. The EF term represents the loss of restoring forces, an independent determinant of e′ in dogs . Its slope was set so that the dilated ventricle, whose relaxation parameter is normal, has an e′ within the clinical range , and in that ventricle the EF term carries the whole fall in e′. Slow relaxation also lowers e′ in heart failure. During pacing-induced heart failure in dogs, τ accounted for 85% of the variance of e′ . Where a low EF and a long τ are combined, the two reductions multiply.

The color M-mode is drawn from the model's mitral velocity and the scaled Vp. The early front travels at a speed chosen so that the slope of the first aliasing contour, with the Nyquist limit at half of E, equals Vp, and the jet core slows toward the apex. The dependence of the measured slope on the Nyquist limit is a property of that construction. In patients, the value of Vp depends on the method by which it is measured . All patients are synthetic, and none of the values is a treatment threshold.
